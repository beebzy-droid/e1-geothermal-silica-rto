To the Editors
Computers & Chemical Engineering

Dear Editors,

I am submitting the manuscript "Hybrid soft sensing and measurement-anchored real-time optimization for silica scaling control in Philippine geothermal fields: a comparison of feedback schemes with out-of-sample validation" for consideration as a full-length article.

The history of this submission should be stated plainly. An earlier version was submitted to Journal of Process Control (JPROCONT-D-26-01181), declined at the desk as outside that journal's scope, and offered for transfer to this journal. The manuscript I am submitting here is not that version. While preparing the transfer I found that the scalar feedback baseline of the earlier version had formed its innovation against the design-case brine composition held by the robust optimizer, whereas the hybrid loop it was compared with had been given the measured silica concentration. With the baseline corrected, the earlier headline claim, that the hybrid loop was the only safe policy, does not hold. I have rewritten the paper around the corrected comparison: it now runs twenty plants per regime with paired confidence intervals, adds a Gaussian-process comparator and a sweep of uncertainty back-off gains, regenerates one table that had been computed before a fix to the plant model, cites the constraint-adaptation and Gaussian-process modifier-adaptation literature the earlier version omitted, and marks in the text which comparisons were registered before their runs and which are post hoc.

The paper applies process systems methods to an energy problem and compares alternative methodologies on it, which is the kind of contribution this journal's scope describes. Silica scaling sets the reinjection temperature floor in the liquid-dominated geothermal fields of the Philippines, and the available rate models are structurally wrong: a classical rate law mispredicts an independent 2024 laboratory dataset sixfold at the median. On a synthetic brine-line plant built from primary-literature chemistry, the paper reports three results. A hybrid soft sensor with a credibility-weighted residual cuts estimation error by half against its kinetic core under sparse daily labels. In closed loop, the anchor of the feedback matters more than the correction model: a constraint modifier whose innovation is formed at the robust optimizer's design-case composition confounds safety margin with model error and incurs about ten times the violation days of the same modifier anchored to the measured silica, and a separate diagnostic reproduces the mechanism. And once the anchor is right, scalar, Gaussian-process and hybrid corrections trace overlapping safety-harvest frontiers; the learned models do not outperform the scalar modifier. The second and third results are negative for the hypothesis the study started with, and the paper says so.

Every experimental number in the manuscript, including the bodies of both tables, is generated by script from result files in a public repository, where a gate of automated checks also verifies that the committed results were produced by the current code. The kinetic, solubility and sensing components are validated out-of-sample against the independent 2024 dataset.

The manuscript is my original work, is not under consideration elsewhere, and I am its sole author. I have no competing interests. My use of a generative AI tool in preparing the manuscript and the software is declared in the manuscript, as the journal requires.

Thank you for considering this work.
