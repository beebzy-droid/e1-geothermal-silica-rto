Silica scaling sets the lowest temperature at which separated brine can be reinjected, and therefore the heat that can be recovered, in the liquid-dominated geothermal fields of the Philippines. Operating nearer that limit needs a scaling-rate model, and available models are structurally wrong: a classical rate law mispredicts an independent 2024 laboratory dataset sixfold at the median. We study how real-time optimization of reinjection temperature and inhibitor dose should be corrected under such mismatch when feedback is one noisy rate label per day. On a synthetic plant built from primary-literature chemistry, a hybrid soft sensor (kinetic core plus a learned residual weighted by its leave-one-out credibility) reduces estimation error by 51% against the kinetic core and 34% against a purely data-driven model. In closed loop the correction model matters less than its anchor. Under a sixfold kinetic error the same scalar constraint modifier incurs 15.80 violation days per 45-day episode when its innovation is formed at the design-case brine composition and 1.60 when formed at the measured silica concentration, because the design-case anchor confounds the robust optimizer's safety margin with model error. Once anchored to the measurement, scalar, Gaussian-process and hybrid corrections trace overlapping safety-harvest frontiers over a sweep of uncertainty back-off gains, and neither learned model outperforms the scalar modifier. The anchoring comparison was added after an earlier version of this study and is reported as post hoc. Kinetic, solubility and sensing components are validated out-of-sample against the 2024 dataset, and every result regenerates from public code.
